Ashwagandha benefits: Ayurvedic medicine and its uses

Ashwagandha is an evergreen shrub plant. This is a well-known herbal plant used in the system of medicine called Ayurveda. Ashwagandha’s scientific name is Withania somnifera. This herbal plant is also called Indian Ginseng or Winter cherry.

Ashwagandha: Overview

The Ashwagandha plant is a medicinal herb widely used for medicinal purposes mostly in Ayurvedic Medicines. The plant’s origins are from India and the Middle East. This also grows in some parts of Africa. From 3,000 years ago it has started including in the preparation of Ayurvedic medicines.

Ayurvedic Practitioner says that it has qualities which improve the overall health. This has started being used in Ayurvedic medicine preparation thousands of years ago.

Clinical trials say that this helps to reduce anxiety and stress. It also improves sexual performance and reduces inflammation in both men and women. India exports ashwagandha extracts and medicine to the United States as well.

Ashwagandha Plant

This is an erect, branching undershrub that reaches about 1.50 m in height. It’s a drought-resistant species with enormous nutrients. It contains green leaves with small yellow flowers and red berries. It grows mainly in dry, sub-tropical regions.

Ashwagandha Producing States

This grows very well in states like Rajasthan, Punjab, Haryana, Uttar Pradesh, Maharashtra, and Madhya Pradesh. Madhya Pradesh cultivates ashwagandha on more than 5000 hectares of land.

How to grow Ashwagandha 

This is an ayurvedic herb plant mostly propagated from seeds. First, you plant fresh seeds in nursery beds that are well-prepared. Ashwagandha can be planted from the seeds in the main field in two ways.

Farmers can scatter them evenly all over the field (broadcast method). Using young plants from the nursery to plant them carefully in the field is called the transplanting method.

  • Nursery Preparation: To cultivate Ashwagandha on a one-acre main field, we typically need to plant almost 10 kilograms of seeds. Prepare the nursery in June and July. During this period, treat the seeds with carbendazim to prevent wilt and seed-borne diseases. The seeds will germinate within 5 to 7 days, When it’s around 35 days old then transplant it into the main fields.
  • Soil: It thrives when cultivated in sandy loam or light red soil. The favorable pH of the soil lies between 7.5 and 8.0 with good drainage. Black soil or heavy soil is suitable for enhanced cultivation. While sowing the seeds, lightly cover them with sandy soil.
  • Climate: The farmers grow this plant after the rainy season. It falls under the Kharif crop variety. It grows very well in semi-tropical areas, where the rainfall is around 500 to 750 mm. It is suitable for cultivation as a rainfed crop. Winter rains mostly affect its root growth. Plant the seeds during the dry season because that’s when the plants grow well.
  • Temperature: Ashwagandha has thermo-tolerance qualities. It grows at a temperature between 200 °C and 380 °C and has low temperatures up to 100°C.

The Field Preparation

  • Ploughing: Plough the land two to three times.
  • Discing: After plowing, use a machine called a disc to break up any big clumps of soil, and it will make it even smoother.
  • Harrowing: This is another step to make the soil nice and soft.
  • Manure: This process helps the plants grow well, up to 10 to 20 tons, with the use of farmyard manure.
  • Leveling: Digging and spreading the land are essential. This helps to make the land even and flat so that planting becomes much easier.
  • Transplanting: After including fertilizers in the soil, they transplant the plants, preparing ridges at a 60-cm spacing. You should plant healthy seedlings with a 30 cm gap between them. The best way to plant them is with a 60 cm by 30 cm gap, which gives around 55,000 seedlings per hectare.
  • Seed treatment: It’s a good way to use Thirum or Dithane M45 (Inofil M45) to protect against diseases. Which is at a rate of 3 grams per kilogram of seeds before planting.
  • Thinning: After seedlings have grown, carefully plant them by scattering them in rows. Thinning is the process of pulling out by hand about 25 to 30 days after sowing. This helps to maintain a plant population of approximately 30 to 60 plants per square meter.
  • Weeding: Farmers need to remove weeds from the field twice. Within 20–25 days of planting, repeat the process after the first weeding.
  • Fertilizers: Ashwagandha does not require heavy doses of fertilizer. This crop does well with natural fertilizers. For growing the plants, use 10 tons of farmyard manure (FYM), which is almost 1 ton of vermicompost per hectare of land. To improve crop yields, it is necessary to apply 15 kg of nitrogen along with 15 kg of phosphorus.
  • Irrigation: Excessive water supply is harmful to Ashwagandha plants. Giving the seedlings water right after planting helps them settle in better. Providing drip irrigation will do wonders. It requires water only once every 10 days.
  • Pest control: There is no serious pest affected by this crop. To protect plants from any pest attacks, one should use Fostion MM pesticides with a concentration of 0.6%. There are several pests, like aphids, mites, and other insects, that will destroy the plants. Farmers should use a mix of 0.5% carbophos and 0.1% and 0.3% dicofol to spray every 10 to 15 days.
  • Disease protection: Diseases like seedling rot will be a problem in hot and humid conditions. To prevent diseases in plants, use seeds that are free from disease. Treat the seeds with carbofuran at a rate of 4-5 kg per acre before planting. Applying neem cake to plants will provide further protection.
  • Harvesting: The plants blossom and the bearing of fruits starts in December. Harvesting Ashwagandha’s roots will start by digging them from January to March. Harvesting happens after 150 to 180 days of sowing of seeds.
  • Yield: In commercial production on one acre of land, farmers can grow about 600 to 1000 kilograms of dry roots. Farmers used to collect almost 100 to 150 kilograms of seeds. In the best conditions, the maximum yield can go up to 13 to 14 quintals per acre.
  • Marketing: Ashwagandha is quite famous in the markets of Madhya Pradesh. Some importers buy within the country.

Dose of Ashwagandha

Ashwagandha plant roots and leaves contain 0.13 to 0.51% alkaloids. Major alkaloids are withanine (sedative), bethenene, withaferin (antitumor), withaferin-A (anti-bacterial), somnine, choline, nicotine, and somniferin.

It has certain nutritious elements like glycosides and vitamins, which are good for health. Along with starch, sugar, and amino acids in Ashwagandha’s extract
